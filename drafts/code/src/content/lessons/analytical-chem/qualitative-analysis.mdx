## Identifying the Mystery

May nakita kang clear solution sa lab. Paano mo malalaman kung ano yung laman nun? Dito papasok ang **Qualitative Analysis**. We don't care how much is there (yet), we just want to know *what* is there.

Sa IChO, qualitative analysis usually comes as a "Spot Test" or a "Systematic Scheme." Kelangan master mo 'to lalo na sa Practical Exam!

## 1. The Systematic Cation Scheme (Fresenius)

Hindi pwedeng random test lang. We use a sequence of reagents para ma-separate yung mga ions by groups.

### Group I: The Chloride Group ($\text{Ag}^+, \text{Pb}^{2+}, \text{Hg}_2^{2+}$)
- **Reagent**: Dilute $\text{HCl}$.
- **Reaction**: Formation of white chloride precipitates (e.g., $\text{AgCl} \downarrow$).
- *Pro-tip*: $\text{PbCl}_2$ is soluble in hot water, so you can separate it from $\text{AgCl}$ and $\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2$ easily!

### Group II: The Acid-Sulfide Group ($\text{Cu}^{2+}, \text{Cd}^{2+}, \text{Bi}^{3+}, \text{As}^{3+}$, etc.)
- **Reagent**: $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ gas in 0.3 M $\text{HCl}$.
- **Logic**: Low solubility product ($K_{sp}$) precipitates form even in acidic conditions.

### Group III: The Basic-Sulfide/Hydroxide Group ($\text{Fe}^{3+}, \text{Al}^{3+}, \text{Cr}^{3+}, \text{Ni}^{2+}, \text{Zn}^{2+}$, etc.)
- **Reagent**: $\text{NH}_3/\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ buffer + $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{S}$.
- **Result**: Mixed hydroxides and sulfides (e.g., $\text{Fe(OH)}_3$ is reddish-brown).

## 2. Flame Tests: The Glow-up

Eto yung pinaka-favorite ng lahat kasi makulay. Pero be careful, accurate description is key.

- **Sodium ($\text{Na}^+$)**: Strong **Persistent Yellow**. (Eto yung epal na ion, kahit konting trace lang, yellow na lahat).
- **Potassium ($\text{K}^+$)**: **Lilac**. 
  - *IChO Requirement*: You **MUST** view this through a **cobalt blue glass** para ma-filter out yung yellow emission ng sodium interferents.
- **Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$)**: **Brick red** (orange-red).
- **Barium ($\text{Ba}^{2+}$)**: **Apple green**.
- **Copper ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$)**: **Blue-green**.

## 3. Confirmatory Tests: Complexation

Para sigurado tayo, we use complex-forming agents.

- **Silver ($\text{Ag}^+$)**: $\text{AgCl}$ dissolves in dilute $\text{NH}_3$ to form the linear complex $[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+$. If you add $\text{HNO}_3$, mag-re-reprecipitate yung $\text{AgCl}$. Logic check!
- **Copper ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$)**: Forms a deep royal blue complex $[\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$ with excess ammonia.

## Level-up Quiz

1. **The Lead Mystery**: Why does $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ appear in both Group I and Group II?
   *Answer: $\text{PbCl}_2$ has a relatively high $K_{sp}$, so it doesn't precipitate completely in Group I.*

2. **The Blue Glass**: Why do we use cobalt blue glass for $\text{K}^+$?
   *Answer: To absorb the $589 \text{ nm}$ emission of Sodium, allowing the faint lilac emission of Potassium to be seen.*
