# Crystal Field Theory and Electronic Spectra

Bakit colorful ang transition metal complexes, habang ang $\text{NaCl}$ solution ay Boring Clear™? Simple: Crystal Field Theory (CFT). Lowkey confusing ang pairing energy at $d$-orbital splitting at first glance, pero logic lang 'yan! Let's master $d$-orbital degeneracy lifting, CFSE, spectrochemical series, and electronic spectra step-by-step.

---

## 🕯🔥 Terms & Definitions 🔥🕯

> **ICHO Grading Tip:** When writing CFSE values for Octahedral fields, explicitly use $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$ labels. Using lower-case or omitting sub-orbital symmetry labels loses points!

### 3.1 Crystal Field Splitting Energy (CFSE) & Barycenter Rule

**Definition:** In an isolated, free gaseous metal ion, all five $d$-orbitals ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}, d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$) are degenerate (equal energy). When point-charge ligands approach the metal center:
- Electrostatic repulsions lift orbital degeneracy.
- The average energy of all five $d$-orbitals remains at the hypothetical **Barycenter** (weighted average energy = 0).

1. **Octahedral Field ($O_h$):**
   - Ligands approach along Cartesian axes ($x, y, z$).
   - Orbitals pointing directly at ligands ($d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$) are destabilized by $+0.6\Delta_o$ ($+3/5 \Delta_o$) $\rightarrow$ **$e_g$ set**.
   - Orbitals pointing between axes ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}$) are stabilized by $-0.4\Delta_o$ ($-2/5 \Delta_o$) $\rightarrow$ **$t_{2g}$ set**.

$$\text{CFSE}_{O_h} = (-0.4 n_{t_{2g}} + 0.6 n_{e_g})\Delta_o + P_{\text{extra}}$$

where $P_{\text{extra}}$ is the additional pairing energy relative to the free ion.

2. **Tetrahedral Field ($T_d$):**
   - Ligands approach between axes.
   - Splitting is inverted: $e$ set is stabilized ($-0.6\Delta_t$), $t_2$ set is destabilized ($+0.4\Delta_t$).
   - Because there are only 4 ligands and none point directly at orbitals:
   $$\Delta_t \approx \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$$
   *(All tetrahedral complexes are High-Spin because $\Delta_t < P$ always!)*

## 🌈 The Spectrochemical Series & Color

Ligands split $d$-orbitals by different magnitudes ($\Delta_o$). Strong-field ligands cause large $\Delta_o$, while weak-field ligands cause small $\Delta_o$:

$$\text{I}^- < \text{Br}^- < \text{S}^{2-} < \text{SCN}^- < \text{Cl}^- < \text{NO}_3^- < \text{F}^- < \text{OH}^- < \text{ox}^{2-} < \text{H}_2\text{O} < \text{NCS}^- < \text{edta}^{4-} < \text{NH}_3 < \text{en} < \text{bpy} < \text{NO}_2^- < \text{CN}^- < \text{CO}$$

### Predicting Complementary Colors
When a complex absorbs light of photon energy $E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \Delta_o$, it transmits the **complementary color**:
- Absorbs **Yellow** ($\sim 580\text{ nm}$) $\rightarrow$ Appears **Violet/Purple**
- Absorbs **Red** ($\sim 700\text{ nm}$) $\rightarrow$ Appears **Green**
- Absorbs **Blue** ($\sim 450\text{ nm}$) $\rightarrow$ Appears **Orange**

> 💡 **Conversion Factor:** $1\text{ cm}^{-1} \approx 0.01196\text{ kJ/mol}$ or $\Delta_o (\text{J}) = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$.

## 💡 Worked Example: High-Spin vs. Low-Spin CFSE Calculation

### Problem
For $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ($d^6$) in an octahedral field, calculate the CFSE in terms of $\Delta_o$ and pairing energy $P$ for both:
1. High-spin complex $\text{[Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$
2. Low-spin complex $\text{[Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$

Given: Mean pairing energy $P = 17,600\text{ cm}^{-1}$.

### Step 0: Figure out the path before you compute
- $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ group 8 metal $\implies 8 - 2 = 6$ $d$-electrons ($d^6$).
- High-spin: Maximize unpaired electrons $\implies t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$.
- Low-spin: Minimize energy by pairing $\implies t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$.

### Step-by-Step Solution

**1. High-Spin Configuration ($t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$):**
- $n_{t_{2g}} = 4$, $n_{e_g} = 2$.
- Electron pairing count in complex = 1 pair (in $t_{2g}$).
- Free ion $d^6$ high-spin (Hund's rule) also has 1 pair $\implies P_{\text{extra}} = 0$.
$$\text{CFSE} = [4(-0.4) + 2(+0.6)]\Delta_o = (-1.6 + 1.2)\Delta_o = \mathbf{-0.4\Delta_o}$$

**2. Low-Spin Configuration ($t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$):**
- $n_{t_{2g}} = 6$, $n_{e_g} = 0$.
- Electron pairing count in complex = 3 pairs.
- Free ion ground state has 1 pair $\implies P_{\text{extra}} = (3 - 1)P = 2P$.
$$\text{CFSE} = [6(-0.4) + 0(+0.6)]\Delta_o + 2P = \mathbf{-2.4\Delta_o + 2P}$$

**Sanity Check:** Low-spin is favored when $\Delta_o > P$. Since $\text{CN}^-$ is at the top of the spectrochemical series, $\Delta_o \gg P$, making $\text{[Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$ strongly low-spin!

## 💥 Common IChO Traps & Grader Callouts

> ⚠️ **TRAP #1: Why $d^0$ and $d^{10}$ Complexes are Colorless**
> $\text{Sc}^{3+}$ ($d^0$), $\text{Ti}^{4+}$ ($d^0$), and $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ ($d^{10}$) cannot exhibit $d-d$ electronic transitions because there are either no $d$-electrons to promote ($d^0$) or all $d$-orbitals are completely filled ($d^{10}$). Their solutions are colorless unless Charge Transfer (LMCT/MLCT) occurs (e.g. $\text{MnO}_4^-$ purple due to LMCT)!

> ⚠️ **TRAP #2: Laporte and Spin Selection Rules**
> - **Laporte Rule:** Transitions between orbitals of the same parity ($g \rightarrow g$, like $d \rightarrow d$) are electric-dipole forbidden in centrosymmetric complexes ($O_h$). They gain weak intensity only through **vibronic coupling** (asymmetric vibrations momentarily destroy the center of inversion), which is why octahedral complexes have pale colors!
> - **Spin Rule:** Transitions involving a change in spin multiplicity ($\Delta S \neq 0$) are strictly spin-forbidden. Spin-allowed transitions ($\Delta S = 0$) are significantly more intense.

> 💡 **Advanced Tip:** Tetrahedral complexes lack a center of inversion, so the Laporte rule does not apply. This is why $\text{[CoCl}_4]^{2-}$ (deep blue) has much more intense color than $\text{[Co(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$ (pale pink).

### Tier 2: Level-up

**Problem 2.1**
The complex $\text{[Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$ is diamagnetic, whereas $\text{[CoF}_6]^{3-}$ is paramagnetic with 4 unpaired electrons.
1. Calculate the CFSE (in terms of $\Delta_o$ and $P$) for both complexes.
2. Given that $P = 21,000\text{ cm}^{-1}$ for $\text{Co}^{3+}$, and $\Delta_o$ for $\text{[Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$ is $22,900\text{ cm}^{-1}$, demonstrate quantitatively why $\text{[Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$ is low-spin.

<details>
<summary>Click for Solution & Mark Scheme</summary>

**Solution:**
1. $\text{Co}^{3+}$ is $d^6$.
- $\text{[Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$ (diamagnetic $\implies n=0$ unpaired $e^-\implies$ Low-spin):
  Configuration: $t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$.
  $\text{CFSE} = 6(-0.4)\Delta_o + 2P = \mathbf{-2.4\Delta_o + 2P}$. [2 pts]
- $\text{[CoF}_6]^{3-}$ (paramagnetic with 4 unpaired $e^- \implies$ High-spin):
  Configuration: $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$.
  $\text{CFSE} = [4(-0.4) + 2(0.6)]\Delta_o = \mathbf{-0.4\Delta_o}$. [2 pts]

2. Quantitative check for $\text{[Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$:
Since $\Delta_o = 22,900\text{ cm}^{-1} > P = 21,000\text{ cm}^{-1}$, the field splitting energy exceeds the pairing energy ($\Delta_o > P$).
Net energy gain for low-spin over high-spin:
$$\Delta E = \text{CFSE}_{\text{low}} - \text{CFSE}_{\text{high}} = (-2.4\Delta_o + 2P) - (-0.4\Delta_o) = -2.0\Delta_o + 2P$$
$$\Delta E = -2.0(22,900) + 2(21,000) = -45,800 + 42,000 = -3,800\text{ cm}^{-1}$$
Since $\Delta E < 0$, the low-spin state is thermodynamically more stable by $3,800\text{ cm}^{-1}$ ($\sim 45.4\text{ kJ/mol}$). [2 pts]
</details>

### Tier 3: Boss Fight

**Problem 3.1**
An aqueous solution of $\text{[Ni(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$ is green, absorbing light at $\lambda_{\text{max}} = 790\text{ nm}$ ($12,650\text{ cm}^{-1}$) corresponding to the lowest energy $d-d$ transition ($\Delta_o$). When ethylenediamine ($\text{en}$) is added to form $\text{[Ni(en)}_3]^{2+}$, the solution turns deep royal blue, absorbing at $\lambda_{\text{max}} = 540\text{ nm}$ ($18,520\text{ cm}^{-1}$).

1. Calculate the crystal field splitting energy $\Delta_o$ in $\text{kJ/mol}$ for both complexes.
2. Calculate the shift in splitting energy $\Delta(\Delta_o) = \Delta_{o,\text{en}} - \Delta_{o,\text{H}_2\text{O}}$ in $\text{kJ/mol}$.
3. Explain why $\text{[Ni(en)}_3]^{2+}$ appears blue using complementary color theory and the spectrochemical series.

<details>
<summary>Click for Solution & Mark Scheme</summary>

**Solution:**
1. Energy formula: $E = \frac{hc N_A}{\lambda}$ or using $1\text{ cm}^{-1} = 0.01196\text{ kJ/mol}$:

For $\text{[Ni(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$:
$$\Delta_o = 12,650\text{ cm}^{-1} \times 0.01196\text{ kJ/mol per cm}^{-1} = \mathbf{151.3\text{ kJ/mol}}$$ [2 pts]

For $\text{[Ni(en)}_3]^{2+}$:
$$\Delta_o = 18,520\text{ cm}^{-1} \times 0.01196\text{ kJ/mol per cm}^{-1} = \mathbf{221.5\text{ kJ/mol}}$$ [2 pts]

2. Shift in splitting energy:
$$\Delta(\Delta_o) = 221.5 - 151.3 = \mathbf{70.2\text{ kJ/mol}}$$ [1 pt]

3. Explanation:
- $\text{en}$ is a stronger field ligand than $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ in the spectrochemical series ($\text{H}_2\text{O} < \text{en}$).
- Substituting $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ with $\text{en}$ increases $\Delta_o$, shifting the absorption maximum to shorter wavelengths ($\lambda_{\text{max}}$ decreases from $790\text{ nm}$ in the NIR/red region to $540\text{ nm}$ in the yellow-green region).
- When yellow-green light ($\sim 540\text{ nm}$) is absorbed, the transmitted complementary color is **deep blue/royal blue**. [2 pts]
</details>
