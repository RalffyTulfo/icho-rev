# Brief for Unit 3: Inorganic Chemistry, Lesson 2

## Title
Coordination Chemistry: Nomenclature, Isomerism, and Bonding

## Goal
Teach students IUPAC nomenclature of coordination complexes, structural and stereoisomerism (cis/trans, fac/mer, optical/chirality), and bonding models (Valence Bond Theory vs Crystal Field Theory foundations).

## Syllabus Coverage
- Coordination complexes: central metal, oxidation state, coordination number, ligand types (monodentate, bidentate, polydentate/chelates, ambidentate).
- IUPAC nomenclature rules for coordination compounds.
- Isomerism: structural (linkage, ionization, coordination) and stereoisomerism (geometric: cis/trans, fac/mer; optical: enantiomers in octahedral/tetrahedral complexes).
- Introduction to bonding: Valence Bond Theory (outer vs inner orbital complexes, hybridization like d2sp3 vs sp3d2) and introduction to Crystal Field Theory.

## Difficulty
Intermediate-Advanced (IChO level).

## Required Problems
1. Name complex coordination compounds according to IUPAC rules.
2. Draw and identify all possible stereoisomers for a given complex (e.g. [Co(en)2Cl2]+, [Pt(NH3)2Cl2], [Co(NH3)3(NO2)3]).
3. Determine oxidation state, d-electron count, and predicted magnetic moment (μ_s = sqrt(n(n+2)) BM).

## Failure Modes
- Incorrect IUPAC nomenclature (wrong ligand order, wrong oxidation state).
- Missing or misidentified isomers (especially optical isomers in octahedral complexes).
- Incorrect d-electron count or hybridization assignment.
- Overly simplistic bonding explanations that ignore ligand field effects.
