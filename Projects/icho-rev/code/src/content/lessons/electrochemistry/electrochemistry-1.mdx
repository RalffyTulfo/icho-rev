# Electrochemical Cells and Potentials

Electrochemistry is just chemistry's way of saying "I can power your phone." We're basically hijacking electron transfers to do work. If you don't get this, you're literally in the dark.

## Terms and Definitions Box

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Anode** | Electrode kung saan nagaganap ang **oxidation**; electrons are released. |
| **Cathode** | Electrode kung saan nagaganap ang **reduction**; electrons are accepted. |
| **Electrolyte** | Medium na nagko-conduct ng ions between electrodes. |
| **Salt Bridge** | Barrier na nag-aallow ng ion flow para ma-maintain ang electrical neutrality. |
| **Galvanic (Voltaic)** | Spontaneous cell na nagko-convert ng chemical energy to electrical energy. |
| **Electromotive Force (EMF)** | The potential difference between electrodes; highkey the "voltage". |
| **Standard Potential ($E^\circ$)** | Reduction potential measured under standard conditions (1 M, 1 atm, 298 K). |
| **Faraday Constant ($F$)** | Charge of one mole of electrons $\approx 96,485 \text{ C mol}^{-1}$. |

## Why does this exist?
Bakit mahalaga ang electrochemical cells? Simple: they're everywhere. From the lithium-ion battery in your pocket to the complex biological redox chains in your mitochondria, electron flow is life. In IChO, they love this because it connects pure thermodynamics ($\Delta G$) to measurable physical properties (Voltage). Kung hindi mo alam kung bakit 1.10V ang Zn-Cu cell, lowkey bagsak ka na sa electrochem. It's the bridge between "this reaction might happen" and "this reaction is powering a city."

## 1. Electrochemical Cell Basics

An electrochemical cell is represented in standard notation. Format: **Anode || Cathode**.
$$\text{Zn}(s) | \text{Zn}^{2+}(1 \text{ M}) || \text{Cu}^{2+}(1 \text{ M}) | \text{Cu}(s)$$

- **Anode (Left):** $\text{Zn}(s) \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}(aq) + 2e^-$ (Oxidation)
- **Cathode (Right):** $\text{Cu}^{2+}(aq) + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}(s)$ (Reduction)
- **Overall:** $\text{Zn}(s) + \text{Cu}^{2+}(aq) \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$

### Standard Cell Potential
The standard cell potential ($E^\circ_\text{cell}$) is the difference between the reduction potentials of the two half-cells:
$$E^\circ_\text{cell} = E^\circ_\text{cathode} - E^\circ_\text{anode}$$

For our Zn-Cu cell:
$$E^\circ_\text{cell} = (+0.34 \text{ V}) - (-0.76 \text{ V}) = +1.10 \text{ V}$$

> **Trap:** Standard potentials are **intensive properties**. Kahit i-multiply mo yung half-reaction by 10 para ma-balance, yung $E^\circ$ value stays the same. Huwag maging bida-bida at i-multiply yung voltage.

## 2. Standard Reduction Potentials

Lahat ng potentials are relative to the **Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)**, which we arbitrarily set to $0.00 \text{ V}$.

| Half-reaction | $E^\circ$ (V) |
|---------------|---------------|
| $\text{F}_2 + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{F}^-$ | +2.87 |
| $\text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ + 5e^- \rightarrow \text{Mn}^{2+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$ | +1.51 |
| $\text{Ag}^+ + e^- \rightarrow \text{Ag}$ | +0.80 |
| $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}$ | +0.34 |
| $2\text{H}^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2$ | 0.00 |
| $\text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Zn}$ | -0.76 |
| $\text{Mg}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Mg}$ | -2.37 |
| $\text{Li}^+ + e^- \rightarrow \text{Li}$ | -3.05 |

**Rule of Thumb:** The more positive the $E^\circ$, the more it *wants* to be reduced. The more negative, the more it *wants* to be oxidized.

## 3. The Nernst Equation

Reality is rarely 1 M. For non-standard conditions, we use the Nernst Equation:
$$E = E^\circ - \frac{RT}{nF} \ln Q$$

At $298.15 \text{ K}$, this simplifies to:
$$E = E^\circ - \frac{0.0592}{n} \log_{10} Q$$

### Thermodynamics Connection (IChO Level 3)
Voltage is just Gibbs Free Energy in a suit:
$$\Delta G = -nFE$$
$$\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ = -RT \ln K$$

This means a positive $E^\circ$ implies a negative $\Delta G^\circ$ and a large $K$. Spontaneous as hell.

## Problem Set

### Warm-up (School Level)
1. Predict if the following reaction is spontaneous under standard conditions:
   $$\text{Fe}^{2+}(aq) + \text{Cu}(s) \rightarrow \text{Fe}(s) + \text{Cu}^{2+}(aq)$$
   Given: $E^\circ_{\text{Fe}^{2+}/\text{Fe}} = -0.44 \text{ V}$, $E^\circ_{\text{Cu}^{2+}/\text{Cu}} = +0.34 \text{ V}$.

### Level-up (National Olympiad)
2. Calculate the equilibrium constant $K$ for the Zn-Cu cell at 25°C.
   $$\text{Zn}(s) + \text{Cu}^{2+}(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{Zn}^{2+}(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$$
   Hint: At equilibrium, $E = 0$.

### Boss Fight (IChO Rigour)
3. A concentration cell is constructed with two silver electrodes. One half-cell contains $0.010 \text{ M AgNO}_3$ and the other contains $1.00 \text{ M AgNO}_3$.
   a) Identify the anode and cathode.
   b) Calculate the cell potential at 25°C.
   c) What happens to the potential if we add $\text{NaCl}$ to the $1.00 \text{ M}$ compartment until $[\text{Cl}^-] = 0.10 \text{ M}$? ($K_{sp}(\text{AgCl}) = 1.8 \times 10^{-10}$).

### Warm-up
**Solution:**
- Cathode candidate: $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ($E^\circ = -0.44 \text{ V}$)
- Anode candidate: $\text{Cu}$ ($E^\circ = +0.34 \text{ V}$)
- $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}} = -0.44 - 0.34 = -0.78 \text{ V}$
- Since $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} < 0$, the reaction is **non-spontaneous**.
**Marking:** 1pt for correct formula, 1pt for final value, 1pt for spontaneity conclusion.

### Level-up
**Solution:**
- $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 1.10 \text{ V}$, $n = 2$.
- $E^\circ = \frac{0.0592}{n} \log_{10} K \Rightarrow 1.10 = \frac{0.0592}{2} \log_{10} K$
- $\log_{10} K = \frac{1.10 \times 2}{0.0592} = 37.16$
- $K = 10^{37.16} \approx 1.45 \times 10^{37}$
**Marking:** 2pts for Nernst-equilibrium relationship, 2pts for calculation.

### Boss Fight
**a) Identification:**
- Concentration cells drive towards equal concentration. The $0.010 \text{ M}$ side must increase (oxidation), so it's the **Anode**. The $1.00 \text{ M}$ side must decrease (reduction), so it's the **Cathode**.

**b) Potential:**
- $E = E^\circ - \frac{0.0592}{n} \log_{10} \frac{[\text{Ag}^+]_\text{anode}}{[\text{Ag}^+]_\text{cathode}}$
- $E = 0 - \frac{0.0592}{1} \log_{10} \frac{0.010}{1.00} = 0 - (-0.1184) = +0.118 \text{ V}$

**c) Effect of NaCl:**
- $[\text{Ag}^+]_\text{cathode} = \frac{K_{sp}}{[\text{Cl}^-]} = \frac{1.8 \times 10^{-10}}{0.10} = 1.8 \times 10^{-9} \text{ M}$.
- New $E = 0 - 0.0592 \log_{10} \frac{0.010}{1.8 \times 10^{-9}} = -0.0592 \times 6.74 = -0.399 \text{ V}$.
- The potential flips! The anode and cathode swap roles. Spontaneous direction is now reversed.
**Marking:** 2pts for part (a), 3pts for part (b), 5pts for part (c) including Ksp calculation and logical conclusion.
