# Thermodynamics: Calorimetry and Heat Capacity

Calorimetry is how we actually measure the heat energy changes ($\Delta H$ or $\Delta U$) in a reaction. When a lab experiment produces heat, you don't just "feel" it—you trap it and measure the temperature rise. Para hindi tayo mag-guess, let’s learn the math behind the bucket. 🔥

---

## 🕯🔥 1. Terms & Definitions 🔥🕯

> **ICHO Grading Tip:** Calorimeter constants change with the setup. Always state the "calorimeter constant" ($C_{cal}$) explicitly if the lab setup involves a non-ideal system.

- **Calorimeter**: A device used to measure the heat of chemical reactions or physical changes as well as heat capacity.
- **Specific Heat Capacity ($c$)**: The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 gram of a substance by 1 Kelvin. Units: $J \cdot g^{-1} \cdot K^{-1}$.
- **Molar Heat Capacity ($C_m$)**: The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 mole of a substance by 1 Kelvin. Units: $J \cdot mol^{-1} \cdot K^{-1}$.
- **Calorimeter Constant ($C_{cal}$)**: The heat capacity of the calorimeter itself. It accounts for the heat absorbed by the thermometer, stirrer, and the container walls.
- **Bomb Calorimeter**: A constant-volume calorimeter used to measure heat of combustion at constant volume (so $\Delta U$, not $\Delta H$).

---

## 💥 2. Why Does This Exist? 💥

We can't measure enthalpy ($H$) directly, but we *can* measure temperature change ($\Delta T$). Calorimetry is the bridge between internal molecular energy and macro-scale lab observations. Without calorimetry, we would have no experimental foundation for the values of $\Delta H_f^\circ$ that Hess's Law depends on. 🥀

---

## 3. Derivation from First Principles

### 3.1 Energy Balance ($\Delta U_{system} = 0$)

In an adiabatic calorimeter, heat exchange only happens within the system:

$$
q_{reaction} + q_{solution} + q_{calorimeter} = 0
$$

Since $q = m c \Delta T$ (for liquids/solids) and $q = C_{cal} \Delta T$ (for the calorimeter apparatus):

$$
q_{rxn} = -(m_{sol} c_{sol} \Delta T + C_{cal} \Delta T)
$$

### 3.2 Constant Pressure vs. Constant Volume

- **Constant Pressure (Coffee-cup)**: $q_p = \Delta H$.
- **Constant Volume (Bomb)**: $q_v = \Delta U$.
- Conversion: $\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta(PV) = \Delta U + \Delta n_{gas} RT$.

---

## 4. Worked Example: Reaction Calorimetry

**Problem**: 50.0 mL of 1.0 M HCl is mixed with 50.0 mL of 1.0 M NaOH in a coffee-cup calorimeter. Initial temperature: 22.0 °C. Final temperature: 28.5 °C. Calculate $\Delta H_{rxn}$ per mole of water formed. (Assume solution density = 1.0 g/mL, $c_{sol} = 4.18 J \cdot g^{-1} \cdot K^{-1}$, $C_{cal} = 0.05 kJ \cdot K^{-1}$).

1. **Moles**: $0.050 L \times 1.0 M = 0.050 mol$.
2. **Temperature change**: $\Delta T = 28.5 - 22.0 = 6.5 K$.
3. **Heat absorbed**:
   - $q_{sol} = (100.0 g)(4.18 J/g\cdot K)(6.5 K) = 2717 J$.
   - $q_{cal} = (50 J/K)(6.5 K) = 325 J$.
   - $q_{total} = 2717 + 325 = 3042 J = 3.042 kJ$.
4. **Enthalpy**: $q_{rxn} = -3.042 kJ$.
   - $\Delta H = -3.042 kJ / 0.050 mol = -60.8 kJ \cdot mol^{-1}$.

---

## 💔 5. Common IChO Traps 💔

1. **Volume of solution**: Remember to add the volumes of the reactants if they are mixed (e.g., 50mL + 50mL = 100mL).
2. **Density**: Assuming water density (1.0 g/mL) for aqueous solutions when the problem gives something else (like 1.05 g/mL) is a trap.
3. **Units**: kJ vs J. The heat of reaction is usually kJ/mol, but $mc\Delta T$ is in Joules. Don't divide by 1000 and lose points.
4. **Bomb Calorimeter Work**: If $\Delta n_{gas} \neq 0$, you *must* correct $\Delta U$ to $\Delta H$.

---

## 🎇 6. What Graders Want to See 🎇

1. **System Definition**: Define exactly what is in your calorimeter system ($m_{sol} + C_{cal}$).
2. **Explicit Balance**: Write $q_{rxn} = -q_{surroundings}$ clearly.
3. **Units**: Every line should have proper units ($J$ or $kJ$).
4. **Assumptions**: Explicitly mention "Assuming specific heat of solution is that of water" if stated.

## 🥀 7. Recall Cards 🥀

1. **What is $C_{cal}$?**
   Ans: Heat capacity of the calorimeter apparatus (thermometer, stirrer, etc.).
2. **Difference between coffee-cup and bomb?**
   Ans: Constant pressure ($P$) vs. constant volume ($V$).
3. **Relationship between $\Delta H$ and $\Delta U$?**
   Ans: $\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta(PV) = \Delta U + \Delta n_{gas} RT$.
4. **Is $c$ extensive or intensive?**
   Ans: Intensive.
5. **How to calculate molar enthalpy if $\Delta T$ is known?**
   Ans: $q = mc\Delta T + C_{cal}\Delta T$; $\Delta H = -q/n$.
6. **If reaction is exothermic, what happens to $T_{sol}$?**
   Ans: It increases.
7. **What if the solution isn't water?**
   Ans: Use the density and specific heat provided in the problem.
8. **Does $C_{cal}$ have units?**
   Ans: Yes, $J \cdot K^{-1}$ or $kJ \cdot K^{-1}$.
9. **Is $\Delta H$ for neutralization usually exothermic?**
   Ans: Yes, very.
10. **tf is a bomb calorimeter used for?**
    Ans: Measuring combustion enthalpy at constant volume (internal energy change).
