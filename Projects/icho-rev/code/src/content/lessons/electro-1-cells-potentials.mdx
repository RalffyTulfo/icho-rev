# Electrochemical Cells and Potentials

## Terms & Definitions

- **Oxidation**: Loss of electrons; occurs at the anode.
- **Reduction**: Gain of electrons; occurs at the cathode.
- **Galvanic (Voltaic) Cell**: Electrochemical cell that uses a spontaneous chemical reaction to generate electricity.
- **Electrolytic Cell**: Electrochemical cell that uses electrical energy to drive a non-spontaneous reaction.
- **Electromotive Force (EMF)**: The potential difference between the cathode and anode, representing the maximum voltage a cell can deliver.
- **Standard Reduction Potential ($E^\circ$ )**: The potential of a half-reaction under standard conditions (1 M, 1 atm, 298 K) relative to the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE).

## Why Does This Exist?

Electrochemistry is the bridge between chemical energy and electrical energy. In the IChO, mastering cell notation, potential calculations, and the relationship between $E^\circ$ , $\Delta G^\circ$, and $K$ is fundamental for solving complex quantitative problems and understanding energy storage systems like batteries and fuel cells.

## Derivation from First Principles

### The Nernst Relation and Free Energy
The maximum non-expansion work a system can perform is equal to the change in Gibbs Free Energy:
$w_{max} = \Delta G$

In an electrochemical cell, this work is electrical:
$w_{elec} = -nFE_{cell}$

Thus, at standard conditions:
$\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ_{cell}$

where:
- $n$ is the number of moles of electrons transferred.
- $F$ is Faraday's constant ($\approx 96485$ C/mol).
- $E^\circ_{cell}$ is the standard cell potential ($E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode}$).

### Cell Notation
A shorthand way to represent a cell:
Anode | Anode Electrolyte || Cathode Electrolyte | Cathode
Example: $Zn(s) | Zn^{2+}(aq) || Cu^{2+}(aq) | Cu(s)$

## Worked Example

**Problem**: Calculate the standard cell potential and $\Delta G^\circ$ for the following reaction:
$Zn(s) + Cu^{2+}(aq) \rightarrow Zn^{2+}(aq) + Cu(s)$
Given: $E^\circ(Zn^{2+}/Zn) = -0.76$ V, $E^\circ(Cu^{2+}/Cu) = +0.34$ V.

**Solution**:
1. **Identify half-reactions**:
   - Anode (Oxidation): $Zn(s) \rightarrow Zn^{2+}(aq) + 2e^-$
   - Cathode (Reduction): $Cu^{2+}(aq) + 2e^- \rightarrow Cu(s)$
2. **Calculate $E^\circ_{cell}$**:
   $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode} = 0.34\text{ V} - (-0.76\text{ V}) = 1.10\text{ V}$
3. **Calculate $\Delta G^\circ$**:
   $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ_{cell} = -(2)(96485\text{ C/mol})(1.10\text{ V})$
   $\Delta G^\circ = -212267\text{ J/mol} \approx -212.3\text{ kJ/mol}$

**Sanity Check**: $E^\circ_{cell}$ is positive, so $\Delta G^\circ$ is negative, indicating a spontaneous reaction as expected for a galvanic cell.

## Common IChO Traps

- **Sign errors**: Always subtract anode from cathode ($E_{red} - E_{ox}$).
- **Stoichiometry**: Standard potentials are *intensive* properties; do NOT multiply $E^\circ$ by the stoichiometric coefficients.
- **Reference electrodes**: Ensure you are using reduction potentials. If an oxidation potential is given, flip the sign.

## What Graders Want to See
- Correct assignment of anode and cathode.
- Explicit calculation of $n$.
- Units (V, kJ/mol) included in the final answer.
- Proper use of cell notation symbols (| and ||).
