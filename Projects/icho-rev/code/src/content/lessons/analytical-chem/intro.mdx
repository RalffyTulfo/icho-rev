## Ano ba talaga ang Analytical Chem?

Openers muna tayo: Bakit kailangan natin 'to? Kasi in chemistry, it's not enough to say "may gold dito." We need to know *gaano karami* at *gaano tayo kasigurado*. Analytical chemistry isn't just about mixing solutions; it's the science of measurements. 

Sa IChO, hindi ka lang tatanungin kung anong kulay ng precipitate. Tatanungin ka: "What is the uncertainty of this result at a 95% confidence level?" So, buckle up!

## 1. Accuracy vs. Precision

Madalas 'to mapagpalit, pero critical 'to sa lab.

- **Accuracy**: Gaano kalapit yung measured value mo sa "True Value." (Think: hitting the bullseye).
- **Precision**: Gaano "consistent" yung results mo pag inulit-ulit. (Think: hitting the same spot, even if it's not the bullseye).

### Systematic vs. Random Errors
- **Systematic Errors (Determinate)**: Consistent bias. Kunwari, hindi naka-calibrate yung balance mo. Madaling i-correct kung alam mo yung source.
- **Random Errors (Indeterminate)**: Fluctuations na hindi maiiwasan (noise, temperature changes). Dito pumapasok ang statistics.

## 2. Statistical Treatment of Data

Sa IChO, we assume random errors follow a **Gaussian Distribution**.

### Mean ($\bar{x}$) and Standard Deviation ($s$)
Para sa small data sets:
$$s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}}$$

### Propagation of Uncertainty
If $y = f(a, b, c)$, how does the error in $a, b, c$ affect $y$?

1. **Addition/Subtraction ($y = a + b$ or $y = a - b$)**:
   $$\sigma_y = \sqrt{\sigma_a^2 + \sigma_b^2}$$
2. **Multiplication/Division ($y = a \times b / c$)**:
   $$\frac{\sigma_y}{y} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_a}{a}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\sigma_b}{b}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\sigma_c}{c}\right)^2}$$

*Sample logic: Pag nag-add ka, absolute errors ang gagamitin. Pag nag-multiply, relative errors.*

## 3. Limit of Detection (LOD) and Quantitation (LOQ)

Hindi lahat ng signal ay signal. Minsan, background noise lang yan.

- **LOD**: Smallest concentration na "different" from the blank.
  $$LOD = \frac{3s_{blank}}{m}$$
  *(where $s_{blank}$ is the std dev of the blank and $m$ is the slope of the calibration curve)*.
- **LOQ**: Smallest concentration na pwede na nating i-quantify accurately.
  $$LOQ = \frac{10s_{blank}}{m}$$

## Boss Level Concept: Confidence Intervals

Sa real world, we use the $t$-test para malaman kung "real" yung difference ng dalawang measurements.
$$CI = \bar{x} \pm \frac{ts}{\sqrt{n}}$$

## Warm-up Practice

1. **The Weighing Dilemma**: You weighed a sample three times: 1.002g, 1.005g, 1.001g. If the true value is 1.010g, are you accurate or precise?
   *Answer: High precision (small s), low accuracy (far from 1.010).*

2. **Error Propagation**: You measured a titration volume by subtracting initial (0.05 ± 0.02 mL) from final (25.15 ± 0.02 mL). What is the absolute uncertainty of the volume delivered?
   *Calculation: $\sqrt{0.02^2 + 0.02^2} \approx 0.028$ mL.*
