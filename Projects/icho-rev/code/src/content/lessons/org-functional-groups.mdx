# Functional Groups and Reactivity

Ano ba talaga ang nagpapatakbo sa isang organic reaction? Hindi lang ito basta pagmemorize ng reagents. Ang sikreto ay nasa **electron density**. Sa lesson na ito, titingnan natin kung paano nag-aaway ang mga atoms para sa electrons at kung paano ito nakakaapekto sa "ugali" ng molekula.

## Terms and Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Inductive Effect** | Paghila o pagtulak ng electron density through sigma bonds dahil sa difference sa electronegativity. |
| **Resonance (Mesomeric) Effect** | Delocalization ng electrons sa pi systems (double bonds, lone pairs). Mas malakas 'to kaysa sa inductive effect usually. |
| **Nucleophile** | Electron-rich species. "Nucleus-loving" kasi naghahanap siya ng positive charge para mag-donate ng electrons. |
| **Electrophile** | Electron-poor species. "Electron-loving" kasi kulang siya sa electrons. |
| **pKa** | Inverse log ng acid dissociation constant ($K_a$). Kapag mas mababa ang pKa, mas matapang ang asido. |

## Why does this exist?

Icl, kung hindi natin alam kung nasaan ang electrons, manghuhula lang tayo sa mechanisms. Ang functional groups ang "business end" ng molekula. Sila ang nagdedetermine kung sasabog ba ang compound mo o magiging gamot. Sa IChO, hindi ka lang tatanungin kung ano ang alcohol; tatanungin ka kung bakit mas acidic ang phenol kaysa sa cyclohexanol. (Spoiler: Resonance is the goat).

## Derivations from First Principles

### 1. Inductive Effect (I-effect)
Ang electronegativity difference ($\Delta \chi$) ang bida rito.
- **-I Effect (Electron Withdrawing)**: Groups like $-F, -Cl, -NO_2, -CN$. Hinahila nila ang electrons palayo sa carbon chain.
- **+I Effect (Electron Donating)**: Alkyl groups ($-CH_3, -CH_2R$). Nagtutulak sila ng electrons via hyperconjugation and polarization.

### 2. Resonance Effect (R or M-effect)
Eto 'yung "teleportation" ng electrons.
- Kapag ang lone pair ay conjugated sa pi system, pwede siyang mag-move.
- **Rule of Thumb**: Resonance stabilization is almost always more significant than inductive effects (except for halogens, yung mga weirdo ng periodic table).

### 3. Acidity and Basicity: The CARDIO Rule
Para malaman kung sino ang mas acidic, tignan ang stability ng **Conjugate Base**. Mas stable ang base, mas acidic ang parent.
- **C**harge: Neutral vs Negative.
- **A**tom: Electronegativity at size (F vs I).
- **R**esonance: Delocalization of negative charge.
- **DI**pole (Inductive): Electron withdrawing groups stabilize negative charge.
- **O**rbital: $sp > sp^2 > sp^3$ in terms of electronegativity.

## Worked Example

**Problem**: Rank the following in order of increasing acidity: Ethanol, Phenol, 4-Nitrophenol, and Acetic Acid.

**Step 0: Figure out the path.**
Acidic means it loses $H^+$. We check the stability of the anions: $EtO^-$, $PhO^-$, $O_2N-C_6H_4-O^-$, and $CH_3COO^-$.

**Solution**:
1. **Ethanol ($EtO^-$)**: No resonance, +I effect from ethyl group destabilizes the charge. Least acidic.
2. **Phenol ($PhO^-$)**: Negative charge is delocalized into the benzene ring. Better.
3. **4-Nitrophenol**: Resonance *plus* the -I and -M effect of the $-NO_2$ group. The nitro group pulls electron density like crazy.
4. **Acetic Acid ($CH_3COO^-$)**: The negative charge is shared between two electronegative Oxygens. This resonance is extremely effective.

**Final Rank**: Ethanol < Phenol < 4-Nitrophenol < Acetic Acid.

**Sanity Check**: Does it make sense? Yes. Carboxylic acids are called acids for a reason. Nitro groups are famous for boosting acidity.

## Key Functional Group Transformations

Hindi lang sapat na alam mo ang pangalan; dapat alam mo rin kung paano sila nagbabago. Eto 'yung bread and butter ng synthesis:

1. **Oxidation/Reduction**:
   - Primary Alcohol $\xrightarrow{[O]}$ Aldehyde $\xrightarrow{[O]}$ Carboxylic Acid.
   - Ketone $\xrightarrow{[R]}$ Secondary Alcohol.
2. **Nucleophilic Addition**: Common sa carbonyls ($C=O$). Ang $\delta+$ carbon ay "attackable" ng nucleophiles like Grignard reagents.
3. **Electrophilic Substitution**: Common sa aromatics. Hinahayaan ng ring na mapalitan ang hydrogen basta't may malakas na electrophile.

## Common IChO Traps

- **The Halogen Paradox**: Halogens are -I (withdraw electrons) but +M (donate via lone pairs). In electrophoresis, -I wins for reactivity, but for orientation in EAS, +M wins. Gulo noh?
- **pKa vs pH**: pH is a property of the solution; pKa is a property of the molecule. Huwag paghaluin, please.
- **H-bonding**: Nakakalimutan minsan na ang intramolecular H-bonding (e.g., in o-nitrophenol) can affect acidity and boiling points.

## Problem Set

### Warm-up
1. Which is a stronger base: Ammonia ($NH_3$) or Methylamine ($CH_3NH_3$)? Why?
   <details>
   <summary>Solution</summary>
   Methylamine is stronger. The $-CH_3$ group has a +I effect, increasing electron density on the Nitrogen, making the lone pair more available.
   </details>

### Level-up
2. Explain why the pKa of p-hydroxybenzoic acid is higher than m-hydroxybenzoic acid.
   <details>
   <summary>Solution</summary>
   In the para position, the $-OH$ group can donate electrons via resonance (+M) directly to the carboxylate group, destabilizing it. In the meta position, resonance doesn't reach the carboxylate, so only the inductive effect (-I) works to stabilize it.
   </details>

### Boss Fight: The Ortho Effect
3. Salicylic acid (o-hydroxybenzoic acid) has a pKa of 2.97, while its isomer p-hydroxybenzoic acid has a pKa of 4.58. Account for this massive difference.
   <details>
   <summary>Solution</summary>
   In salicylic acid, the conjugate base (salicylate) is stabilized by a very strong **intramolecular hydrogen bond** between the phenoxide/hydroxyl oxygen and the carboxylate oxygen. This locks the negative charge in a 6-membered ring-like structure, making the loss of $H^+$ extremely favorable.
   </details>
