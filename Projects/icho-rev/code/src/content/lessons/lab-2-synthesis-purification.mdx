# Synthesis, Purification, and Recrystallization

## Terms & Definitions

1. **Recrystallization**: A technique for purifying solid compounds by dissolving them in a suitable solvent at high temperature and then cooling to promote the formation of pure crystals.
2. **Yield**: The ratio of the actual amount of product obtained to the theoretical maximum, expressed as a percentage.
3. **Thin-Layer Chromatography (TLC)**: A technique used to monitor reaction progress and determine purity by separating components based on polarity using a stationary phase.
4. **Drying Agent**: An anhydrous inorganic salt (e.g., $MgSO_4, Na_2SO_4$) used to remove residual water from organic solvents.
5. **Mother Liquor**: The solvent remaining after crystals have formed, containing impurities and a small amount of product.

## Why Does This Exist?

In the IChO practical exam, synthesizing a target molecule is only half the battle. You must obtain a high yield and high purity. Mastering purification techniques like recrystallization and monitoring progress via TLC is the difference between a high-scoring sample and a disqualified one.

## Core Techniques

### 1. Recrystallization Solvent Choice
1. **Solubility Rule**: Select a solvent where the compound is highly soluble when hot and minimally soluble when cold.
2. **Impurities**: Impurities should either remain highly soluble in the cold solvent or be insoluble in the hot solvent (removed via hot filtration).
3. **Polarity**: Match the polarity (e.g., polar compounds for polar solvents like ethanol/water; non-polar for solvents like hexane).

### 2. Yield Calculations
1. **Theoretical Yield**: Based on the limiting reagent.
2. **Actual Yield**: Mass of dry, purified product.
3. **Percent Yield**: $(\frac{\text{Actual Yield}}{\text{Theoretical Yield}}) \times 100\%$

### 3. Monitoring with TLC
1. **$R_f$ Value**: Retention factor = distance traveled by spot / distance traveled by solvent front.
2. **Monitoring**: Use TLC to determine if the starting material is fully consumed. If starting material spots persist, the reaction is incomplete.

## Worked Example

**Problem**: You synthesize 5.00g of aspirin (Theoretical Yield). After recrystallization, you recover 4.25g of pure crystals. Calculate the percent yield and describe how to verify its purity using TLC.

**Solution**:
1. **Percent Yield**: $(\frac{4.25\text{g}}{5.00\text{g}}) \times 100\% = 85.0\%$.
2. **TLC Verification**: Dissolve a small amount of crude and recrystallized aspirin. Run a TLC plate with a known pure aspirin standard. If the recrystallized sample shows a single spot matching the standard and the crude shows secondary spots, the purification was successful.

## Common IChO Traps

1. **Over-heating**: Boiling off solvent until it dry-up, losing product.
2. **Too much solvent**: Using excess solvent prevents crystallization upon cooling.
3. **Too fast cooling**: Cooling too rapidly traps impurities inside the crystal lattice.

## Recall Cards
- **Recall Card 1**: What is the formula for $R_f$?
  Answer: distance of spot / distance of solvent front.
- **Recall Card 2**: Why do we use anhydrous salts?
  Answer: To remove residual water from organic phases.
