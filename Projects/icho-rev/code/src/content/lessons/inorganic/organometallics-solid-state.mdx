# Organometallics, Catalysis, and Solid-State Chemistry

Lowkey, organometallics is just fancy electron counting while solid-state chemistry is basically Minecraft in real life. If you can handle the 18-electron rule and visualize a BCC unit cell without losing your mind, you're halfway to an IChO gold. Let's get it.

---

## 🕯🔥 Terms & Definitions 🔥🕯

> **ICHO Grading Tip:** When counting electrons for organometallic complexes, always state your method (neutral atom vs. ionic). Stick to ONE method throughout your answer to avoid double-counting or missing ligands!

### 4.1 The 18-Electron Rule
Most stable organometallic complexes with strong-field ligands ($\text{CO}, \text{PR}_3, \text{CN}^-$) have 18 valence electrons (a full $nd, (n+1)s, (n+1)p$ shell).

**Electron Counting (Neutral Method):**
- Metal: Group number = valence $e^-$ count.
- Ligands: Count donated electrons (e.g., $\text{CO} = 2e^-, \text{Cl} = 1e^-, \text{Cp} = 5e^-$).

### 4.2 Synergic Back-Bonding
$\text{CO}$ is a strong $\sigma$-donor and $\pi$-acceptor. 
- **$\sigma$-donation:** $\text{CO}$ lone pair to empty metal $d$-orbital.
- **$\pi$-backbonding:** Metal $d$-electrons flow into $\text{CO}$ $\pi^*$ (antibonding) orbitals.
- **Result:** $M-C$ bond strengthens (shorter $M-C$ length), $\text{C-O}$ bond weakens (lower IR frequency $\nu_{\text{CO}}$).

---

## 🛠 Catalytic Cycles (Simplified)

1. **Oxidative Addition:** Metal oxidation state and coordination number increase by 2.
2. **Migratory Insertion:** Ligand moves into a metal-ligand bond (e.g. $\text{R}$ migrates to $\text{CO}$).
3. **Reductive Elimination:** Metal oxidation state and coordination number decrease by 2.

## 💡 Worked Example: Solid-State Density

### Problem
Calculate the density ($d$) of gold (FCC structure, atomic radius $r = 144\text{ pm}$, molar mass $M = 197.0\text{ g/mol}$).

### Step 0: Figure out the path
- FCC: $Z = 4$ atoms/cell. Edge length $a = \frac{4r}{\sqrt{2}} = 2\sqrt{2}r$.
- $V = a^3$. Density $d = \frac{ZM}{V N_A}$.

### Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Edge length ($a$):** $a = 2\sqrt{2} \times 144\text{ pm} = 407.3\text{ pm} = 4.073 \times 10^{-8}\text{ cm}$.
2. **Volume ($V$):** $V = (4.073 \times 10^{-8})^3 = 6.75 \times 10^{-23}\text{ cm}^3$.
3. **Density ($d$):**
   $d = \frac{4 \times 197.0}{(6.75 \times 10^{-23}) \times (6.022 \times 10^{23})} = 19.4\text{ g/cm}^3$.

<details>

### Tier 2: Level-up
**Problem 2.1** Explain why $\nu_{\text{CO}}$ decreases as the metal oxidation state decreases.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
Lower metal oxidation state $\rightarrow$ metal is more electron-rich $\rightarrow$ stronger $\pi$-backbonding $\rightarrow$ more electron density in $\text{CO}$ $\pi^*$ orbital $\rightarrow$ weaker $\text{C-O}$ bond $\rightarrow$ lower $\nu_{\text{CO}}$. [2 pts]
</details>

### Tier 3: Boss Fight
**Problem 3.1** A metal $\text{M}$ forms an FCC lattice. $\lambda_{\text{X-ray}} = 154\text{ pm}$. Diffraction from (111) plane at $\theta = 19.2^\circ$. Calculate density.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
Bragg's Law: $2d\sin\theta = \lambda \implies d_{111} = 234\text{ pm}$. $a = d \sqrt{3} = 405\text{ pm}$. Use $Z=4$ and $M$ to find $d$. [4 pts]
</details>
