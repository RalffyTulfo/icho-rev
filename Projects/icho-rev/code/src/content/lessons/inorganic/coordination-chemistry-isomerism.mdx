# Coordination Chemistry: Nomenclature, Isomerism, and Bonding

Isomerism sa coordination compounds is one of the favorite playgrounds of IChO problem setters. If you miss a single optical isomer or mess up the IUPAC name by putting ligands in the wrong alphabetical order, say goodbye to those 4.0 points. Let's make sure you never fall for these traps again.

---

## 🕯🔥 Terms & Definitions 🔥🕯

> **ICHO Grading Tip:** IUPAC nomenclature rules for coordination complexes were updated! Ligands are ALWAYS named in alphabetical order regardless of their charge (anionic, neutral, or cationic). Don't use old textbook rules where anionic ligands come first!

### 2.1 Coordination Complex & Ligands

**Definition (IUPAC):** A coordination complex consists of a central metal atom or ion bound to surrounding molecules or ions known as **ligands** via coordinate covalent (dative) bonds.

- **Monodentate ligands:** Bind via a single donor atom (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ aqua, $\text{NH}_3$ ammine, $\text{Cl}^-$ chlorido, $\text{CN}^-$ cyanido).
- **Bidentate / Polydentate ligands (Chelates):** Bind via two or more donor atoms simultaneously (e.g., ethylenediamine $\text{en}$, oxalate $\text{ox}^{2-}$, $\text{EDTA}^{4-}$). Chelation provides extra thermodynamic stability due to positive chelate entropy ($\Delta S^\circ > 0$).
- **Ambidentate ligands:** Can coordinate through two different donor atoms (e.g., $\text{NO}_2^-$ coordinates via N to give *nitro* or via O to give *nitrito*).

### 2.2 Stereoisomerism in Octahedral & Square Planar Complexes

1. **Geometric Isomerism ($cis/trans$ and $fac/mer$):**
   - **$cis/trans$**: In square planar $[\text{MA}_2\text{B}_2]$ or octahedral $[\text{MA}_4\text{B}_2]$, identical ligands occupy adjacent ($90^\circ$, $cis$) or opposite ($180^\circ$, $trans$) sites.
   - **$fac/mer$**: In octahedral $[\text{MA}_3\text{B}_3]$, if 3 identical ligands occupy a facial triangle, it's **$fac$** ($C_{3v}$ local symmetry). If they span a meridian circle around the metal center, it's **$mer$** ($C_{2v}$ local symmetry).

2. **Optical Isomerism (Chirality):**
   - A complex is chiral (optically active) if it lacks an improper rotation axis $S_n$ (which includes a center of inversion $i = S_2$ and mirror plane $\sigma = S_1$).
   - Classic example: Tris-bidentate complexes $[\text{M(en)}_3]^{n+}$ or $cis-[\text{M(en)}_2\text{B}_2]^{n+}$ exist as non-superimposable mirror image pairs ($\Delta$ and $\Lambda$ enantiomers).

## 🛠 IUPAC Nomenclature Rules

Follow these exact steps when naming any coordination complex:

1. **Cation first, anion second** (standard ionic naming).
2. **Inside the coordination sphere:** Name ligands in **alphabetical order** (ignoring prefixes like di-, tri-, bis-, tris- unless the ligand itself contains a numerical prefix).
3. **Ligand endings:**
   - Anionic ligands end in **-o** (e.g., chloride $\rightarrow$ chlorido, hydroxide $\rightarrow$ hydroxido, cyanide $\rightarrow$ cyanido).
   - Neutral ligands retain their name, except: $\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow$ aqua, $\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow$ ammine, $\text{CO} \rightarrow$ carbonyl, $\text{NO} \rightarrow$ nitrosyl.
4. **Metal name:**
   - If the complex sphere is a **cation** or **neutral**, use the standard element name (e.g., cobalt, iron, copper).
   - If the complex sphere is an **anion**, add the suffix **-ate** to the metal name (e.g., cobaltate, ferrate, cuprate, argentate, aurate, plumbate).
5. **Oxidation State:** Indicate the oxidation state of the central metal as a Roman numeral in parentheses right after the metal name.

## 💡 Worked Example: Isomer Identification & Naming

### Problem
Consider the neutral complex $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_3(\text{NO}_2)_3]$.
1. Give the IUPAC name for this compound.
2. Draw all possible stereoisomers and classify them as $fac$ or $mer$.
3. Determine if any of the stereoisomers are optically active.

### Step 0: Figure out the path before you compute
- Central metal: $\text{Co}$ with neutral $\text{NH}_3$ and anionic $\text{NO}_2^-$ ligands.
- Overall charge = 0 $\implies \text{Co}^{3+}$ ($d^6$).
- Formula is $[\text{MA}_3\text{B}_3]$ octahedral $\implies$ expect $fac$ and $mer$ geometric isomers.

### Step-by-Step Solution

**1. IUPAC Naming:**
- Ligands: ammine (3) and nitrito-$\kappa\text{N}$ (3).
- Note: "Nitro" is a common nickname for $\text{NO}_2^-$ attached via N, but IUPAC prefers "nitrito-$\kappa\text{N}$".
- Alphabetical order: ammine comes before nitrito / nitro.
- Neutral complex sphere $\implies$ cobalt(III).
- **Official IUPAC Name:** triamminetrinitrito-$\kappa\text{N}$-cobalt(III).

**2. Stereoisomer Breakdown:**
- **$fac$-isomer (facial):** The 3 $\text{NH}_3$ ligands occupy one face of the octahedron (positions 1, 2, 3), and the 3 $\text{NO}_2^-$ ligands occupy the opposite face (positions 4, 5, 6).
- **$mer$-isomer (meridional):** The 3 $\text{NH}_3$ ligands lie in a single plane passing through the cobalt center (positions 1, 2, 4).

**3. Optical Activity Check:**
- $fac-[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_3(\text{NO}_2)_3]$ possesses a $C_{3v}$ point group, which includes 3 vertical mirror planes $\sigma_v$. Mirror plane present $\implies$ **achiral (optically inactive)**.
- $mer-[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_3(\text{NO}_2)_3]$ possesses a $C_{2v}$ point group, which includes 2 mirror planes. Mirror plane present $\implies$ **achiral (optically inactive)**.

**Sanity Check:** For any $[\text{MA}_3\text{B}_3]$ complex with monodentate ligands, there are exactly 2 geometric isomers ($fac$ and $mer$), and both are achiral!

## 💥 Common IChO Traps & Grader Callouts

> ⚠️ **TRAP #1: Forgetting that $trans-[\text{M(en)}_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$ is Achiral!**
> Students often assume that all complexes with bidentate chelates are chiral. But $trans-[\text{Co(en)}_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$ has a plane of symmetry ($\sigma_h$) perpendicular to the $C_2$ axis, plus an inversion center $i$. It is **achiral**! Only the $cis-[\text{Co(en)}_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$ isomer is chiral ($\Delta$ and $\Lambda$).

> ⚠️ **TRAP #2: Incorrect d-Electron Counting in High-Spin vs. Low-Spin Complexes**
> Always calculate $d$-electrons using:
> $$n_d = \text{Group Number} - \text{Oxidation State}$$
> For $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ (Group 8): $n_d = 8 - 3 = 5$ ($d^5$). Spin-only magnetic moment $\mu_s$:
> $$\mu_s = \sqrt{n(n+2)} \quad \text{BM}$$
> High-spin $d^5$ ($n=5$ unpaired $e^-$): $\mu_s = \sqrt{5(7)} = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92\text{ BM}$.
> Low-spin $d^5$ ($n=1$ unpaired $e^-$): $\mu_s = \sqrt{1(3)} = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.73\text{ BM}$.

> ⚠️ **TRAP #3: Linkage Isomers with Ambidentate Ligands**
> IChO often tests linkage isomerism using $\text{SCN}^-$ (thiocyanato-$\kappa\text{S}$) vs $\text{NCS}^-$ (isothiocyanato-$\kappa\text{N}$). Even if the formula looks the same, the point group and properties can change! For example, $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{SCN}]^{2+}$ vs $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{NCS}]^{2+}$. Always check which atom is the donor.

### Tier 2: Level-up

**Problem 2.1**
Draw all possible stereoisomers (geometric and optical) for the octahedral complex ion $[\text{Co}(\text{en})\text{NH}_3(\text{H}_2\text{O})\text{Cl}_2]^+$. State which pairs are enantiomers.

<details>
<summary>Click for Solution & Mark Scheme</summary>

**Solution:**
Ethylenediamine ($\text{en}$) must occupy $cis$ positions ($90^\circ$).
Let $\text{en}$ occupy positions (1,2).
- **Case 1: $trans$-chlorido.** The two $\text{Cl}^-$ ligands occupy positions 3 and 6 ($180^\circ$). $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ occupy positions 4 and 5. This isomer has a mirror plane $\implies$ **achiral** (1 isomer).
- **Case 2: $cis$-chlorido.** The two $\text{Cl}^-$ ligands occupy $90^\circ$ positions (e.g. 3,4).
  - Subcase 2a: $\text{NH}_3$ is $trans$ to one $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is $trans$ to the other $\text{Cl}^-$. This structure lacks $S_n \implies$ **chiral pair ($\Delta/\Lambda$ enantiomers)** (2 isomers).
  - Subcase 2b: $\text{NH}_3$ is $trans$ to one donor nitrogen of $\text{en}$. This structure also lacks $S_n \implies$ **chiral pair ($\Delta/\Lambda$ enantiomers)** (2 isomers).

Total stereoisomers = **5** (1 achiral $trans$-dichloro + 2 pairs of $cis$-dichloro enantiomers). [4 pts]
</details>

### Tier 3: Boss Fight

**Problem 3.1**
A transition metal complex $\text{X}$ has the empirical formula $\text{PtCl}_4 \cdot 2\text{NH}_3$.
1. Electrical conductivity measurements show that 1 mole of $\text{X}$ produces 3 moles of ions in aqueous solution. Write the coordination formula and IUPAC name of $\text{X}$.
2. Determine the magnetic moment ($\mu_s$) of the central metal ion assuming square planar vs. octahedral geometry.
3. Draw all stereoisomers of $\text{X}$ and determine which ones are optically active.

<details>
<summary>Click for Solution & Mark Scheme</summary>

**Solution:**
1. empirical formula $\text{PtCl}_4 \cdot 2\text{NH}_3$. Doubling gives $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]\text{Cl}_2$ where central metal is $\text{Pt(IV)}$ ($d^6$).
Formula: **$[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]\text{Cl}_2$** (tetraamminedichloridoplatinum(IV) chloride). Dissociates into 1 $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]^{2+}$ and 2 $\text{Cl}^- \implies$ 3 ions! [2 pts]

2. $\text{Pt}^{4+}$ is $5d^6$. All $5d$ transition metal complexes are **low-spin** due to large crystal field splitting $\Delta_o$.
In octahedral low-spin $d^6$, all 6 electrons fill the $t_{2g}$ orbitals paired ($\mathbf{t_{2g}^6 e_g^0}$).
Unpaired electrons $n = 0 \implies \mathbf{\mu_s = 0\text{ BM}}$ (diamagnetic). [2 pts]

3. $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]^{2+}$ is $[\text{MA}_4\text{B}_2]$ octahedral:
- **$cis$-isomer:** $90^\circ$ $\text{Cl}-\text{Pt}-\text{Cl}$. Has a mirror plane $\sigma_v \implies$ **achiral**.
- **$trans$-isomer:** $180^\circ$ $\text{Cl}-\text{Pt}-\text{Cl}$. Has a mirror plane $\sigma_h$ and inversion center $i \implies$ **achiral**.
None of the stereoisomers are optically active! [2 pts]
</details>
