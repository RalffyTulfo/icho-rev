# Organic Chemistry: Carbonyl Chemistry and Pericyclic Reactions

## Terms & Definitions

- **Enolate**: Resonance-stabilized carbanion formed by deprotonation alpha to a carbonyl group.
- **Aldol Addition / Condensation**: Nucleophilic addition of an enolate to an aldehyde or ketone, followed by dehydration under thermal conditions.
- **Pericyclic Reaction**: Concerted reaction proceeding through a cyclic transition state with no discrete intermediates.
- **Electrocyclic Reaction**: Ring closure or opening involving the conversion of a pi bond to a sigma bond (or vice versa) via thermal or photochemical activation.
- **Cycloaddition (e.g., Diels-Alder)**: Concerted combination of two or more unsaturated molecules to form a cyclic compound.
- **Sigmatropic Rearrangement**: Migration of a sigma-bonded group within a pi-electron system (e.g., Cope and Claisen rearrangements).

## Why Does This Exist?

Carbonyl chemistry enables carbon-carbon bond formation via enolates, while pericyclic reactions provide stereospecific pathways governed by orbital symmetry (Woodward-Hoffmann rules). Both are indispensable for total synthesis in IChO.

## Derivation from First Principles

### Woodward-Hoffmann Rules for Pericyclic Reactions
Pericyclic reactions are controlled by the symmetry of the Highest Occupied Molecular Orbital (HOMO) of the reactants:
- **[4+2] Diels-Alder Cycloaddition**: Thermal reaction between a conjugated diene (4 pi electrons) and a dienophile (2 pi electrons). The reaction is thermally allowed because the HOMO of the diene has the same symmetry phase as the LUMO of the dienophile (suprafacial-suprafacial).
- **Electrocyclic Reactions**: 
  - 4n electrons: Conrotatory thermal, disrotatory photochemical.
  - 4n+2 electrons: Disrotatory thermal, conrotatory photochemical.

## Worked Example

## Step 0: Identify Diene and Dienophile
**Problem**: Predict the stereochemical outcome and structure of the Diels-Alder adduct formed between (2E,4E)-hexa-2,4-diene and maleic anhydride (cis-but-2-enedioic anhydride).

**Solution**:
1. **Diene**: (2E,4E)-hexa-2,4-diene has terminal methyl groups pointing outward (both E).
2. **Dienophile**: Maleic anhydride is cis-substituted.
3. **Stereospecificity**: Diels-Alder cycloaddition is concerted and cis-addition with respect to the dienophile and diene.
4. **Product**: Because the diene is (2E,4E), the methyl groups end up cis to each other on the newly formed cyclohexene ring, pointing endo or exo depending on transition state stabilization. The reaction obeys the Alder endo rule, favoring the endo transition state where electron-withdrawing carbonyls align with diene pi-systems.

**Sanity Check**: Concerted [4+2] cycloaddition preserves the cis-geometry of maleic anhydride in the resulting bicyclic anhydride adduct.

## Common IChO Traps

1. **Concerted vs Stepwise**: Do not draw carbocation intermediates for Diels-Alder or pericyclic reactions; they are concerted processes.
2. **Endo Rule**: Remember that electron-withdrawing substituents on the dienophile preferentially orient endo in the transition state.

## Recall Cards
- **Recall Card 1**: What is the stereospecificity of the Diels-Alder reaction regarding the dienophile?
  Answer: It is strictly stereospecific; cis-dienophiles yield cis-substituted cyclohexenes, and trans-dienophiles yield trans-substituted cyclohexenes.
- **Recall Card 2**: What thermal electrocyclic ring closure is allowed for a 4n+2 polyene?
  Answer: Disrotatory ring closure.
