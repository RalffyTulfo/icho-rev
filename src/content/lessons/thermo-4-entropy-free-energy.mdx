# Thermodynamics: Entropy and Free Energy

## Terms & Definitions

- **Second Law of Thermodynamics**: The entropy of an isolated system always increases over time for a spontaneous process.
- **Spontaneity**: The tendency of a process to occur without outside intervention.
- **Entropy ($S$)**: A measure of the number of microstates available to a system, defined by Boltzmann's equation: $S = k_B \ln \Omega$.
- **Third Law of Thermodynamics**: The entropy of a pure crystalline substance approaches zero as the temperature approaches absolute zero ($T \rightarrow 0$).
- **Gibbs Free Energy ($G$)**: A thermodynamic potential that measures the maximum reversible work obtainable from a system at constant temperature and pressure: $G = H - TS$.
- **Van 't Hoff Equation**: An equation relating the temperature dependence of the equilibrium constant ($K$) to the standard enthalpy change ($\Delta H^\circ$) of a reaction: $\ln \frac{K_2}{K_1} = -\frac{\Delta H^\circ}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_1}\right)$.


## Why Does This Exist?

While the First Law ensures energy conservation, it does not determine the direction of a process. The Second and Third Laws of Thermodynamics introduce entropy, which dictates the direction of spontaneous processes. Gibbs Free Energy combines enthalpy and entropy to predict whether a process will occur spontaneously at constant temperature and pressure. This lesson covers entropy, Gibbs free energy, and temperature dependence.


## Derivation from First Principles

### Second Law and Entropy

The Second Law states that for any spontaneous process, the total entropy of the system and its surroundings increases:

$$ \Delta S_{total} = \Delta S_{sys} + \Delta S_{surr} > 0 $$

Entropy is defined statistically through Boltzmann's formula:

$$ S = k_B \ln \Omega $$

where $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant and $\Omega$ is the number of microstates.

For a reversible process, the change in entropy is:

$$ dS = \frac{dq_{rev}}{T} $$

### Third Law of Thermodynamics

The Third Law states that as temperature approaches absolute zero ($T \rightarrow 0$), the entropy of a pure, perfectly crystalline substance approaches zero:

$$ \lim_{T \to 0} S = 0 $$

### Gibbs Free Energy

Gibbs Free Energy ($G$) is defined as:

$$ G = H - TS $$

For a process at constant temperature and pressure, the change in Gibbs free energy is:

$$ \Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S $$

- **Spontaneous Process**: $\Delta G < 0$
- **Non-spontaneous Process**: $\Delta G > 0$
- **Equilibrium**: $\Delta G = 0$

### Temperature Dependence and Van 't Hoff Equation

The standard Gibbs free energy change is related to the equilibrium constant $K$ by:

$$ \Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K $$

Combining this with $\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$, we get the Van 't Hoff equation, which relates the change in equilibrium constant with temperature:

$$ \ln \frac{K_2}{K_1} = -\frac{\Delta H^\circ}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_1}\right) $$


## Worked Example

**Problem**: Calculate the change in Gibbs free energy ($\Delta G$) for a reaction at 298 K, given $\Delta H = -50 \, \text{kJ/mol}$ and $\Delta S = -100 \, \text{J/mol}\cdot\text{K}$. Determine if the reaction is spontaneous.

**Solution**:

Using the Gibbs free energy equation:

$$ \Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S $$

Convert $\Delta S$ to kJ/mol·K:

$$ \Delta S = -100 \, \text{J/mol}\cdot\text{K} = -0.1 \, \text{kJ/mol}\cdot\text{K} $$

Substitute the given values:

$$ \Delta G = -50 \, \text{kJ/mol} - (298 \, \text{K} \times -0.1 \, \text{kJ/mol}\cdot\text{K}) $$

$$ \Delta G = -50 + 29.8 = -20.2 \, \text{kJ/mol} $$

**Sanity Check**: Since $\Delta G$ is negative ($\Delta G < 0$), the reaction is spontaneous at 298 K.


## Common IChO Traps

1. **Units of Entropy**: Entropy is typically given in J/mol·K, whereas enthalpy is often given in kJ/mol. Ensure units are consistent before calculating $\Delta G$.

2. **Temperature Units**: Always use Kelvin for temperature in thermodynamic calculations.

3. **Sign of $\Delta G$**: Remember that a negative $\Delta G$ indicates a spontaneous process, while a positive $\Delta G$ indicates a non-spontaneous process.
