## Terms and Definitions

- **IUPAC Nomenclature**: Systematic naming system for organic compounds based on parent chain, substituents, and functional groups.
- **Stereoisomers**: Compounds with the same molecular formula and connectivity but different spatial arrangement.
- **E/Z Isomerism**: Geometric isomerism in alkenes based on substituent priority.
- **R/S Configuration**: Absolute configuration of chiral centers using Cahn-Ingold-Prelog rules.
- **Meso Compound**: Achiral compound with chiral centers that results in an internal plane of symmetry.
- **Conformational Isomerism**: Different spatial arrangements of atoms that can interconvert by rotation around single bonds.
- **Chair Conformation**: Most stable conformation of cyclohexane, minimizing torsional strain.
- **Newman Projection**: View along a carbon-carbon bond showing front and back groups.

## Why does this exist?

In organic chemistry, naming compounds correctly is like giving them a proper name so everyone knows what you're talking about. Imagine trying to order medicine without knowing the exact name of the drug! Isomerism is even more important because the same molecular formula can lead to very different properties and behaviors. For example, two isomers of limonene smell like lemon and orange, but they are completely different compounds. This unit helps you master the language of organic chemistry and understand how small changes in structure can lead to big differences in function.

## Derivations from First Principles

### IUPAC Nomenclature

1. **Parent Chain**: Identify the longest continuous carbon chain containing the highest priority functional group.
2. **Substituents**: Number the chain to give substituents the lowest possible numbers.
3. **Functional Groups**: Use prefixes and suffixes to indicate functional groups (e.g., -ol for alcohols, -oic acid for carboxylic acids).

**Example**: 
- CH3-CH2-CH2-CH3 → Butane
- CH3-CH=CH-CH3 → But-2-ene
- CH3-CH2-OH → Ethanol

### Stereoisomerism

1. **E/Z Isomerism**: Assign priority to substituents on each carbon of the double bond. Higher priority groups on the same side = Z isomer; opposite sides = E isomer.

2. **R/S Configuration**: Assign priorities to substituents on a chiral center using atomic number. Clockwise = R, counterclockwise = S.

### Conformational Isomerism

1. **Chair Conformations**: Cyclohexane prefers the chair conformation to minimize torsional strain. Axial substituents are perpendicular to the ring plane, while equatorial substituents are roughly parallel.

2. **Newman Projections**: Visualize the rotation around a carbon-carbon bond to identify eclipsed, staggered, and gauche conformations.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Naming a Compound

**Structure**: CH3-CH2-CH(CH3)-CH=CH2

**Step 0: Figure out the path before you compute.**

1. Identify the longest chain: 5 carbons (pentane base)
2. Locate the double bond: C4-C5
3. Number the chain to give the double bond the lowest number: 1-pentene
4. Identify and number substituents: methyl group at C3

**Final Name**: 3-Methyl-1-pentene

### Example 2: Determining R/S Configuration

**Structure**: CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-CH3

**Step 0: Figure out the path before you compute.**

1. Identify the chiral center: C2
2. Assign priorities to substituents:
   - OH (1st priority)
   - CH3 (2nd priority)
   - CH2-CH3 (3rd priority)
   - H (4th priority)
3. Orient the lowest priority group (H) away from you
4. Trace the path from 1st to 2nd to 3rd priority: clockwise = R

**Configuration**: R

## Common IChO Traps

1. **Incorrect Parent Chain**: Choosing the wrong longest chain can lead to incorrect names.
2. **Misidentifying Stereoisomers**: Forgetting to check for E/Z or R/S isomerism can lead to missing important compounds.
3. **Conformational Analysis Errors**: Ignoring axial/equatorial preferences can lead to incorrect stability predictions.
4. **Spectroscopic Misinterpretation**: Misreading IR or NMR spectra can lead to incorrect isomer identification.
