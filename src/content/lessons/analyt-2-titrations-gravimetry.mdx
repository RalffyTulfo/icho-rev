# Analytical Chemistry: Titrations, Indicators, and Gravimetric Analysis

## Terms & Definitions

- **Equivalence Point**: The theoretical point in a titration where moles of added titrant exactly equal moles of analyte according to stoichiometry.
- **End Point**: The experimental point where an indicator changes color or a physical sensor signals titration completion.
- **Titration Error**: The difference between the end point and the equivalence point.
- **pH Indicator**: A weak organic acid or base whose protonated and deprotonated forms possess distinct colors.
- **Gravimetric Analysis**: Quantitative analytical technique where the analyte is converted to an insoluble precipitate, filtered, dried, and weighed.
- **Chelometric Titration**: Titration using a chelating agent (e.g., EDTA) that forms 1:1 complexes with metal ions regardless of charge.

## Why Does This Exist?

Titrimetric and gravimetric analyses are classical quantitative pillars of analytical chemistry. Mastering titration curves, indicator selection, and stoichiometric error analysis is crucial for IChO practical and theoretical problem-solving.

## Derivation from First Principles

### Titration Curve for Weak Acid with Strong Base
Consider titrating Va liters of weak acid HA (Ca) with strong base (Cb).
- **Before equivalence point**: A buffer region forms consisting of remaining HA and generated A-. Use Henderson-Hasselbalch.
- **At equivalence point**: All HA is converted to A-. The solution acts as a weak base:
  Kb = Kw / Ka = [OH-][HA] / [A-]
  Solving for [OH-] yields [OH-] = sqrt(Kb * [A-]), from which pOH and pH are calculated.

## Worked Example

## Step 0: Stoichiometry and Equivalence Point Calculation
**Problem**: Calculate the pH at the equivalence point when 25.0 mL of 0.100 M acetic acid (CH3COOH, Ka = 1.8e-5) is titrated with 0.100 M NaOH.

**Solution**:
1. **Moles at equivalence**:
   - Moles CH3COOH initially = 0.0250 L * 0.100 M = 2.50e-3 mol.
   - Requires 2.50e-3 mol NaOH added.
   - Volume of NaOH added = 2.50e-3 mol / 0.100 M = 0.0250 L = 25.0 mL.
   - Total volume at equivalence = 25.0 + 25.0 = 50.0 mL = 0.0500 L.
2. **Concentration of acetate (CH3COO-)**:
   - [CH3COO-] = 2.50e-3 mol / 0.0500 L = 0.0500 M.
3. **Calculate Kb of acetate**:
   - Kb = Kw / Ka = 1.0e-14 / 1.8e-5 = 5.56e-10.
4. **Calculate [OH-] and pH**:
   - [OH-] = sqrt(Kb * [CH3COO-]) = sqrt((5.56e-10)(0.0500)) = 5.27e-6 M.
   - pOH = -log(5.27e-6) = 5.278.
   - pH = 14.000 - 5.278 = 8.72.

**Sanity Check**: The equivalence point of a weak acid titrated with a strong base is basic (pH > 7) due to hydrolysis of the conjugate base, matching 8.72.

## Common IChO Traps

1. **Indicator Selection**: Choose an indicator whose color transition range overlaps the steep inflection region of the titration curve at the equivalence point.
2. **EDTA Titrations**: Remember that EDTA forms 1:1 complexes with metal ions regardless of charge, and conditional formation constants (Kf prime) depend strongly on auxiliary buffer pH.

## Recall Cards
- **Recall Card 1**: Is the equivalence point of a weak acid-strong base titration acidic, neutral, or basic?
  Answer: Basic (pH > 7), because the conjugate base of the weak acid undergoes hydrolysis to produce OH-.
- **Recall Card 2**: What criteria govern a good acid-base indicator?
  Answer: Its color change interval (pKin +/- 1) must coincide with the steep vertical pH inflection range at the equivalence point.
