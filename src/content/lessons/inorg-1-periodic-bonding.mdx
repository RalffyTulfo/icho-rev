# Inorganic Chemistry: Periodic Trends and Advanced Bonding

## Terms & Definitions

- **Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)**: The net positive charge experienced by valence electrons, accounting for electron shielding: Zeff = Z - S.
- **Electron Affinity**: The enthalpy change when an electron is added to a gaseous atom or ion.
- **Molecular Orbital (MO) Theory**: Bonding model where atomic orbitals combine linearly (LCAO) to form delocalized molecular orbitals spanning the entire molecule.
- **Bond Order**: Half the difference between the number of bonding and antibonding electrons: BO = (Nb - Na) / 2.
- **VSEPR Theory**: Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion model predicting molecular geometry based on minimizing electrostatic repulsion between electron domains.
- **Walsh's Diagrams**: Correlation diagrams showing how molecular geometry changes with the number of valence electrons.

## Why Does This Exist?

Periodic trends and molecular orbital theory explain why elements combine in specific stoichiometries and geometries. Advanced bonding models clarify magnetic properties, bond lengths, and stability that simple Lewis structures fail to predict (e.g., paramagnetic O2).

## Derivation from First Principles

### Molecular Orbital Theory for Diatomic Molecules
For homonuclear diatomics of the second period (B2 to Ne2), atomic orbitals combine to form sigma and pi MOs. 
- For B2, C2, and N2, s-p orbital mixing is significant, pushing the sigma_2pz orbital above the pi_2px,y orbitals.
- For O2 and F2, s-p mixing is negligible, placing sigma_2pz below pi_2px,y.
- **Paramagnetism of O2**: With 12 valence electrons, O2 has two unpaired electrons in the degenerate pi*2p antibonding orbitals, confirming its experimentally observed paramagnetism.

## Worked Example

## Step 0: Count Valence Electrons and Write MO Configuration
**Problem**: Calculate the bond order of the superoxide ion (O2-) and state whether it is paramagnetic or diamagnetic.

**Solution**:
1. **Valence electron count**: Oxygen atom has 6 valence electrons * 2 = 12 electrons, plus 1 extra electron for the negative charge = 13 valence electrons.
2. **MO Filling Order** (for O2 manifold where sigma_2pz is lower than pi_2p):
   - sigma_1s^2 sigma*_1s^2 sigma_2s^2 sigma*_2s^2 sigma_2pz^2 pi_2px^2 pi_2py^2 pi*_2px^2 pi*_2py^1
3. **Bonding vs Antibonding count**:
   - Bonding electrons (Nb) = 2 (sigma_2s) + 2 (sigma_2pz) + 4 (pi_2p) = 8.
   - Antibonding electrons (Na) = 2 (sigma*_2s) + 3 (pi*_2p) = 5.
4. **Bond Order calculation**:
   BO = (8 - 5) / 2 = 1.5
5. **Magnetic property**: There is 1 unpaired electron in pi*_2p, so O2- is **paramagnetic**.

**Sanity Check**: Superoxide has a weaker bond than neutral O2 (bond order 2.0) because of the extra antibonding electron, matching the decrease in bond order to 1.5.

## Common IChO Traps

1. **s-p Mixing**: Remember that s-p mixing occurs in B2, C2, N2, changing the MO filling order compared to O2 and F2.
2. **Octet Rule Exceptions**: Period 3 and heavier elements can expand their octets utilizing vacant d orbitals (or more accurately, 3-center 4-electron hypervalent bonding).

## Recall Cards
- **Recall Card 1**: Why is O2 paramagnetic according to MO theory?
  Answer: Because its two highest occupied electrons reside in degenerate pi* antibonding orbitals with parallel spins (Hund's rule).
- **Recall Card 2**: How does bond order relate to bond length and bond strength?
  Answer: Higher bond order corresponds to shorter bond length and greater bond dissociation energy (strength).
