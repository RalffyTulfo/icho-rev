# Analytical Chemistry: Acid-Base Equilibria and Buffer Systems

## Terms & Definitions

- **Brønsted-Lowry Acid / Base**: Proton (H+) donor / proton acceptor.
- **Autoionization Constant (Kw)**: Equilibrium constant for water dissociation: Kw = [H+][OH-] = 1.0e-14 at 25 degrees Celsius.
- **Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation**: Mathematical expression relating pH to buffer component concentrations: pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA]).
- **Buffer Capacity (beta)**: The ability of a buffer solution to resist pH changes, defined as the moles of strong acid or base required to change the pH of 1 liter of buffer by 1 unit: beta = dn/d(pH).
- **Polyprotic Acid**: An acid capable of donating multiple protons per molecule (e.g., H3PO4).

## Why Does This Exist?

Precise control of pH is mandatory in titrations, biological assays, and chemical synthesis. Buffer calculations and fractional composition (alpha) curves are staple analytical problems in IChO theoretical and laboratory exams.

## Derivation from First Principles

### The Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation
Consider a weak acid HA dissociating in water:
HA <-> H+ + A-, Ka = [H+][A-] / [HA]
Taking the negative logarithm (log) of both sides:
log Ka = log[H+] + log([A-]/[HA])
-pKa = -pH + log([A-]/[HA])
pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA])

## Worked Example

## Step 0: Write Equilibrium Expressions and Mass Balance
**Problem**: Calculate the pH of a buffer solution prepared by mixing 50.0 mL of 0.100 M acetic acid (CH3COOH, Ka = 1.8e-5) with 30.0 mL of 0.100 M sodium hydroxide (NaOH).

**Solution**:
1. **Moles of components**:
   - Moles CH3COOH = 0.050 L * 0.100 M = 5.00e-3 mol.
   - Moles OH- (NaOH) = 0.030 L * 0.100 M = 3.00e-3 mol.
2. **Neutralization reaction**:
   CH3COOH + OH- -> CH3COO- + H2O
   - Remaining CH3COOH = 5.00e-3 - 3.00e-3 = 2.00e-3 mol.
   - Formed CH3COO- = 3.00e-3 mol.
   - Total volume = 50.0 + 30.0 = 80.0 mL = 0.0800 L.
3. **Concentrations in buffer**:
   - [CH3COOH] = 2.00e-3 / 0.0800 = 0.0250 M.
   - [CH3COO-] = 3.00e-3 / 0.0800 = 0.0375 M.
4. **Henderson-Hasselbalch calculation**:
   - pKa = -log(1.8e-5) = 4.745.
   - pH = 4.745 + log(0.0375 / 0.0250) = 4.745 + log(1.50) = 4.745 + 0.176 = 4.92.

**Sanity Check**: Since moles of conjugate base exceed weak acid, the pH is higher than pKa (4.75), which is correct.

## Common IChO Traps

1. **Volume Additivity**: Always recalculate concentrations using the total combined volume after mixing solutions.
2. **Buffer Range**: Buffers function effectively within pH = pKa +/- 1. Outside this range, buffer capacity drops precipitously.

## What Graders Want to See
- Clear mole balance before and after neutralization.
- Correct use of Henderson-Hasselbalch equation with proper concentration ratios.
- Final pH reported to correct significant figures (decimal places match sig figs of concentration).
