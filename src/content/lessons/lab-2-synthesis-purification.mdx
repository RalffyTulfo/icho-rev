# Practical Lab Skills: Synthesis, Extraction, and Purification

## Terms & Definitions

- **Recrystallization**: Purification technique where a solid is dissolved in a minimum amount of hot solvent and allowed to slowly crystallize upon cooling.
- **Liquid-Liquid Extraction**: Transfer of a solute between two immiscible phases based on partition coefficients (KD = [Solute]org / [Solute]aq).
- **Vacuum Filtration**: Rapid filtration technique using a Büchner funnel and suction flask powered by a vacuum aspirator.
- **Melting Point Range**: The temperature interval from when the first crystal begins to melt until the entire sample is liquid; sharp melting points indicate high purity.
- **Chromatography (TLC / Column)**: Separation technique based on differential partitioning between a stationary phase and a mobile phase.

## Why Does This Exist?

Synthetic organic and inorganic chemistry requires isolating pure products from complex reaction mixtures. Mastering extraction efficiency, recrystallization solvent selection, and melting point analysis is essential for IChO practical lab success.

## Derivation from First Principles

### Liquid-Liquid Extraction Efficiency
The fraction of solute remaining in the aqueous phase (q) after n successive extractions with volume V_org of organic solvent, starting from aqueous volume V_aq, is given by:
q^n = (V_aq / (V_aq + KD * V_org))^n
Extraction efficiency is significantly higher when performing multiple successive extractions with smaller volumes rather than a single extraction with the total volume.

## Worked Example

## Step 0: Apply Extraction Efficiency Formula
**Problem**: An organic compound has a partition coefficient KD = [org] / [aq] = 4.0 between diethyl ether and water. You have 1.0 g of the compound dissolved in 100 mL of water. Compare the mass extracted into the organic phase using (a) a single extraction with 100 mL of ether, versus (b) two successive extractions with 50 mL of ether each.

**Solution**:
1. **Case (a): Single extraction with 100 mL**:
   - V_aq = 100 mL, V_org = 100 mL, KD = 4.0.
   - Fraction remaining in water (q) = 100 / (100 + (4.0 * 100)) = 100 / 500 = 0.20.
   - Mass remaining in water = 1.0 g * 0.20 = 0.20 g.
   - Mass extracted = 1.0 - 0.20 = 0.80 g.
2. **Case (b): Two successive extractions with 50 mL each (n=2)**:
   - V_aq = 100 mL, V_org = 50 mL, KD = 4.0.
   - Fraction remaining in water (q^2) = (100 / (100 + (4.0 * 50)))^2 = (100 / 300)^2 = (1/3)^2 = 1/9 approx 0.111.
   - Mass remaining in water = 1.0 g * 0.111 = 0.111 g.
   - Mass extracted = 1.0 - 0.111 = 0.889 g.

**Sanity Check**: Two extractions with 50 mL each extract 0.889 g, which is greater than 0.80 g extracted by a single 100 mL portion, proving the superiority of multiple extractions.

## Common IChO Traps

1. **Venting Separatory Funnels**: Always invert and vent separatory funnels frequently during extractions to release volatile solvent vapor pressure.
2. **Drying Agents**: Ensure wet organic extracts are dried over anhydrous drying agents (MgSO4, Na2SO4) before solvent evaporation.

## Recall Cards
- **Recall Card 1**: Why are multiple small-volume extractions more efficient than a single large-volume extraction?
  Answer: Because extraction efficiency depends exponentially (q^n) on the number of successive extractions (n), leaving progressively less solute behind.
- **Recall Card 2**: What does a broad or depressed melting point range indicate about a synthesized solid?
  Answer: It indicates the presence of impurities, which disrupt crystal lattice formation and lower the melting point range.
