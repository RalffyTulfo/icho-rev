# Organic Chemistry: Reaction Mechanisms (SN1, SN2, E1, E2, Additions)

## Terms & Definitions

- **Nucleophile**: Electron-rich chemical species with an unshared electron pair donating electrons to an electrophile.
- **Electrophile**: Electron-deficient species seeking electron density.
- **SN2 Mechanism**: Bimolecular nucleophilic substitution; concerted single-step backside attack with complete stereochemical inversion.
- **SN1 Mechanism**: Unimolecular nucleophilic substitution proceeding via a planar carbocation intermediate with racemic product formation.
- **E2 Mechanism**: Bimolecular elimination requiring anti-periplanar geometry between the leaving group and beta-proton.
- **Markovnikov's Rule**: In electrophilic addition to unsymmetrical alkenes, the electrophile adds to the carbon with more hydrogens, forming the more stable carbocation.

## Why Does This Exist?

Reaction mechanisms form the core of synthetic organic chemistry in IChO. Predicting whether a substrate undergoes substitution (SN1/SN2) or elimination (E1/E2) based on nucleophile strength, solvent polarity, and substrate structure is essential for multi-step synthesis design.

## Derivation from First Principles

### Kinetics and Rate Laws
- **SN2**: Rate = k[Substrate][Nucleophile]. Second-order overall. Stereospecific inversion (Walden inversion).
- **SN1**: Rate = k[Substrate]. First-order overall. Carbocation intermediate leads to racemization.
- **E2**: Rate = k[Substrate][Base]. Requires anti-periplanar alignment (theta = 180 degrees) of H and leaving group to allow maximum overlap of sigma C-H bonding orbital with sigma* C-LG antibonding orbital.

## Worked Example

## Step 0: Identify Substrate and Reagents
**Problem**: Predict the major organic product and mechanism when (2S)-2-bromobutane is treated with sodium methoxide (NaOCH3) in methanol at room temperature.

**Solution**:
1. **Substrate**: Secondary alkyl halide (2-bromobutane).
2. **Reagent**: Methoxide ion (CH3O-) is a strong nucleophile and strong base in a protic polar solvent.
3. **Competing Pathways**: SN2 vs E2. With a secondary substrate and strong hindered/unhindered base, E2 and SN2 compete. Methoxide is relatively small and basic, favoring a mixture where E2 (Zaitsev elimination yielding 2-butene) dominates at higher temperatures, alongside SN2 inversion to (2R)-2-methoxybutane.
4. **Major Product**: 2-butene (predominantly trans-2-butene via anti-periplanar E2) and (2R)-2-methoxybutane.

**Sanity Check**: Strong base + secondary substrate = prominent E2 elimination alongside SN2.

## Common IChO Traps

1. **Carbocation Rearrangements**: Always check for 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methide shifts whenever a carbocation intermediate (SN1, E1) is formed to generate a more stable tertiary or resonance-stabilized carbocation.
2. **Zaitsev vs Hofmann**: Bulky bases (e.g., potassium tert-butoxide) abstract the less hindered proton yielding Hofmann elimination products, whereas small bases give Zaitsev (more substituted alkene).

## Recall Cards
- **Recall Card 1**: What is the stereochemical outcome of an SN2 reaction?
  Answer: Complete inversion of configuration (Walden inversion) at the electrophilic carbon.
- **Recall Card 2**: What geometric requirement must be met for an E2 elimination?
  Answer: The beta-hydrogen and the leaving group must be anti-periplanar (180 degree dihedral angle).
