# Thermodynamics: Hess's Law and Enthalpy of Formation

## Terms & Definitions

- **Hess's Law**: The enthalpy change for a reaction is the same regardless of the pathway taken. It can be expressed as the sum of enthalpy changes for individual steps in a reaction pathway.
- **Standard Enthalpy of Formation ($[3m\Delta H_f^[0m$)**: The enthalpy change when one mole of a substance is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states.
- **Bond Dissociation Enthalpy**: The energy required to break one mole of bonds in a gaseous molecule.
- **Born-Haber Cycle**: A thermodynamic cycle used to determine lattice enthalpies of ionic compounds.


## Why Does This Exist?

Hess's Law is a powerful tool for calculating enthalpy changes for complex reactions by breaking them down into simpler, known steps. Understanding standard enthalpies of formation and bond dissociation enthalpies allows us to predict the enthalpy changes for various chemical processes. This lesson introduces Hess's Law, standard enthalpies of formation, bond dissociation enthalpies, and the Born-Haber cycle.


## Derivation from First Principles

### Hess's Law

Hess's Law is derived from the First Law of Thermodynamics and the fact that enthalpy is a state function. It states that the enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the pathway taken. Mathematically, if a reaction can be represented as a series of steps, the overall enthalpy change is the sum of the enthalpy changes for each step:

$$[3m\Delta H_{rxn} = \sum \Delta H_{steps}[0m$$

### Standard Enthalpy of Formation

The standard enthalpy of formation ($[3m\Delta H_f^[0m$) is defined as the enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their standard states. By definition, the standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state is zero.

### Bond Dissociation Enthalpy

Bond dissociation enthalpy is the energy required to break a bond in a gaseous molecule. It is related to the enthalpy change of a reaction through the bonds broken and formed:

$$[3m\Delta H_{rxn} = \sum \Delta H_{bonds broken} - \sum \Delta H_{bonds formed}[0m$$

### Born-Haber Cycle

The Born-Haber cycle is a thermodynamic cycle used to determine the lattice enthalpy of an ionic compound. It involves the following steps:

1. Sublimation of the metal.
2. Ionization of the metal.
3. Dissociation of the non-metal.
4. Formation of ions.
5. Formation of the ionic solid.

The overall enthalpy change for the formation of the ionic compound can be calculated using the enthalpy changes for each step in the cycle.


## Worked Example

**Problem**: Calculate the standard enthalpy of formation of water ($[3m\Delta H_f^[0m$) using the following data:
- $\Delta H_f^[0m$ of H₂ (g) = 0 J/mol
- $\Delta H_f^[0m$ of O₂ (g) = 0 J/mol
- Bond dissociation enthalpy of H-H = 436 kJ/mol
- Bond dissociation enthalpy of O=O = 498 kJ/mol
- Bond formation enthalpy of O-H = -463 kJ/mol

**Solution**:

The reaction for the formation of water is:

$$[3m\frac{1}{2} H_2 (g) + rac{1}{2} O_2 (g) \rightarrow H_2O (l)[0m$$

Using bond dissociation and formation enthalpies:

$$[3m\Delta H_{rxn} = \left(\frac{1}{2} \times 436 \, \text{kJ/mol} + \frac{1}{2} \times 498 \, \text{kJ/mol}\right) - \left(2 \times 463 \, \text{kJ/mol}\right)[0m$$

$$[3m\Delta H_{rxn} = (218 + 249) - 926 = -459 \, \text{kJ/mol}[0m$$

**Sanity Check**: The calculated enthalpy change is negative, indicating that the formation of water from its elements is exothermic, which is consistent with known thermodynamic data.


## Common IChO Traps

1. **Pathways**: Ensure that you correctly identify and sum the enthalpy changes for all steps in the reaction pathway.

2. **Sign Conventions**: Be careful with the signs of bond dissociation and formation enthalpies. Bond breaking is endothermic (positive), while bond formation is exothermic (negative).

3. **Stoichiometry**: Ensure that the stoichiometric coefficients are correctly applied when summing enthalpy changes.
